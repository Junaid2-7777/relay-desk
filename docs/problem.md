# Help with the errand you still have to remember

**“The shop said it would refund me. Can you help me check on Friday?”** The return is already at the shop. The person who took it back still has to remember to look for the money, work out who to ask if it hasn’t arrived, and decide when they can put the matter down.

Relay Desk is a proposed personal assistant for anyone with a loose end like that. Someone could start by typing one sentence on their phone. If the shop has written to them, they can paste just the relevant line, with personal details removed. Relay shows what it understood and lets them correct it. Together they agree on a next check. Friday is the day this person wants to look, even if the shop never promised a date.

That first errand works with the information the person chose to share. Relay can keep the promise, show what is still missing and help word a question for the shop. With no permission to read a settlement record, it must tell the person to check for the credit themselves. A pasted promise cannot verify that the money arrived. The person can stop tracking whenever they choose. A calendar connection or a spending limit only becomes relevant if they later ask Relay to book something or pay a bill.

## A Monday with the desk

Later, the same person might trust Relay with more. On **Monday 23 November 2026, India Standard Time**, imagine three updates arriving after they have separately allowed Relay to check a refund, book a visit and handle bills for one account. Every amount, account, communication and calendar entry in this example is synthetic.

| What the owner handed over | What the desk should do | When the owner hears from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recover ₹1,249 for a return.** The merchant says it has issued the refund. | Keep the refund open and check the authorized settlement feed for an incoming credit matching this return, account, currency and amount. Keep the merchant’s promise with the file. | A pending refund can wait quietly until its agreed checkpoint. A missed promise may warrant one specific follow-up for review. Show a completed refund only after the matching credit posts. |
| **Book one free broadband visit on Tuesday afternoon.** Existing household commitments are protected. | The provider offers 11 am and 3 pm on 24 November. The earlier slot overlaps an existing event and falls outside the grant. Recheck the free 3 pm slot, book it with the provider, then enter the confirmed visit in the calendar. | The booking goes into the brief with the confirmation and calendar entry. Ask the owner if every remaining choice would require a fee, a different window or moving another commitment. |
| **Pay this account’s electricity bills up to ₹900.** The current unpaid invoice changes from ₹850 to ₹1,180 before any payment. | Compare the new invoice with the standing limit. Hold payment and put the original amount, new amount, payee and bill reference together. | **Pay ₹1,180 once** or **Leave unpaid**. The exception belongs to this invoice revision. If approved, wait for the biller’s matching settled receipt. The ₹900 limit still governs the next bill. |

Only the bill needs an answer that morning. The person can decide it or leave it unpaid. Underneath, they can find “Broadband booked for Tuesday at 3 pm” and “Watching for your ₹1,249 credit.” Each line opens to the record that supports it. ## Give it something new

Someone else might start with “Track whether the library closed my old membership.” Agree on the membership reference, the issuing library, the allowed reads and the record that counts as closure. A reply acknowledging the request keeps the errand open. A matching closure record from the designated library can finish it. If Relay cannot access that record, it should say what is missing. Tracking the request leaves the decision to cancel with the person.

The scholarship specialist applies the same principle to an already-filed NMMSS application. Official notices can move its school and district deadlines. A school’s acknowledgement of a student correction proves receipt. School verification hands the case to the district. The case finishes on the district’s own verification record. ## What we know and what we need to learn

A published survey of 2,243 UK adults documented time and well-being costs across ten areas of everyday administration, including bills and goods and services. It gives us a reason to investigate this work. Demand in India, the kinds of help people would trust, and any time saved by Relay Desk remain open questions. [Everyday administrative burdens and inequality](https://doi.org/10.1111/puar.13709)

For a first qualitative study, invite five adults who recently waited on a shop, office or service provider. Include people who keep track in their head or on paper and people who use apps. Ask each how their last errand went and what finally told them it was settled. Use a fictional return for the walkthrough so nobody has to share personal messages or account details.

Give each person a blank screen and that one errand. Let them type, use a redacted sample, or decline to share it. Observe whether they can save a next check unaided, tell who needs to look for the credit, and explain what Relay can see. Someone who thinks connecting an account is compulsory or assumes the refund has been verified has found a problem to fix. Record the exact point where they needed help. Then offer the Monday brief as a separate exercise for anyone interested in booking or payments. Ask what would happen to the next bill after paying ₹1,180 once.

Report first-use effort, unwanted interruptions and refusals separately from technical scores. Before any pilot with live records, establish the permitted evidence source and how the person can withdraw access. User interviews, usability results and provider partnerships are still to come.
